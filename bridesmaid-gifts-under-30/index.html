A bridesmaid thank-you under $30 should be something she would keep if the wedding were already over. A silk sleep mask, a plain tote, a good pair of socks, or a small charger will get used. A vinyl tumbler with a nickname will not.

The short version. Buy the same object, or the same kind of object, for everyone in the group, in a size or scent you can actually get right. Write a note that is about her, not about her role. Give the gifts before the wedding morning — at a dinner, a quiet gathering, or by mail — so the thank-you is not another task on the day. Skip monograms unless you know she wants one, and skip anything she has to wear in photographs in order for the gift to make sense.

Top picks

  • Best under $15: a silk sleep mask, a jewelry pouch, a pocket journal, or a set of claw clips.
  • Best around $20 to $30: a slim charger, a compact umbrella, a pack of good socks, or a simple tumbler.
  • Best if you are bundling: fashion tape or a lint roller added to a slightly larger gift, not asked to carry the whole thank-you.
  • Useful kit: the small, plain objects below — things she would buy herself, without her title on the side.

What this gift is for

A bridesmaid thank-you is easy to overthink and just as easy to cheapen. The job of the gift is to acknowledge real work — time, travel, a dress, a weekend — without pretending that a $25 object can repay it. Useful and specific beats expensive and generic, especially when you are buying for a group.

The guide to useful wedding-party gifts covers a wider budget and a wider set of roles. This list is for the common, tight ceiling: under $30, and still something she will keep. If you can spend more, spend it on her travel, her hair appointment, or a better version of one object, not on adding a second, worse object to the bag.

Give everyone in the group the same kind of gift so no one is left doing comparison. Sizes, scents, and colors can vary if you actually know them; the category should not. A note that mentions a specific thing she did will do more work than a monogram. If you also need a way to share logistics, a bridesmaid information card belongs in the planning, not in the thank-you box as a substitute for a present.

Timing matters more than wrapping. A gift handed over at a dinner a week or two before, or shipped to her house, will be remembered more kindly than a box she has to manage in a getting-ready room. If Christmas is when you are all together, it is a perfectly good time to say thank you early.

What to skip

Skip the robe she is required to wear in photos in order for the gift to “work.” Skip a tumbler with a nickname she does not use. Skip jewelry you would not wear yourself, and skip a candle in a scent you have not smelled. Skip anything over the $30 ceiling you set and then inflating it with shipping and a bag of crinkle paper until the gesture feels bigger than it is.

Do not ask her to be surprised and grateful in front of a camera. Give the gift, give her time to open it, and let the photograph, if there is one, be optional. If your budget is tight, a sincere note and one good object are enough. A pile of dollar-store extras will not hide a missing thank-you.

A short gift checklist

Will she still use this in March? Did you buy the same kind of gift for everyone? Is the note actually about her? If you do not know her size, do not buy clothing. If you do not know her scent, do not buy perfume.

Order early enough that you can replace a size or a color without panic. Write the notes before the weekend gets loud. Then let the thank-you be finished, so she can go back to being your friend and not a role.

Questions people actually ask

What are good bridesmaid gifts under $30?

A silk sleep mask, a plain tote, a lip mask, a slim charger, a compact umbrella, a pack of good socks, a jewelry pouch, or a simple tumbler. The useful test: would she keep it if the wedding were already over?

Do I have to spend the same amount on each bridesmaid?

Spend in the same range, and give the same kind of gift. A maid of honor can receive a slightly larger version of the same object, or a separate thank-you for extra work, but she should not open an obviously different category in front of the group.

When should I give bridesmaid gifts?

Before the wedding morning. A dinner, a quiet gathering, or a package at her house is better than a box she has to guard while her hair is being done. A holiday get-together is a perfectly good time if that is when you are all in one place.

Are monograms a good idea on a small gift?

Only if you know she likes them, and if you can get the name or initials right. A monogram makes a tote or a pouch harder to use later if the style is not hers. Plain is safer under $30.

What if $30 feels too small for what she has done?

Say that in the note, and, if you can, spend on her travel, her lodging, or her appointment instead of stretching the object. A modest, useful gift plus a specific thank-you is better than an expensive object that misses.
