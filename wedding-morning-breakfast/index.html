A Wedding-Morning Breakfast That Doesn't Need a Short-Order Cook

Wedding-morning breakfast is food for the people getting ready and the family already in the house, not a rehearsal of the reception. A make-ahead vegetarian strata, baked until the center is hot, with an optional meat pan that is labeled as its own dish, will feed a crowd without asking someone in a wedding outfit to stand at a skillet.

The short version. Feed the people who will be in the house that morning — the wedding party, parents, a photographer who arrived early — not the entire guest list. Assemble a vegetarian spinach and roasted-pepper strata the night before, bake it in the morning until the center reaches 160°F, and hold it hot, at or above 140°F, if people will eat over a long getting-ready window. If you want a meat version, bake it in a separate, labeled pan, not as a strip of sausage at one end of the vegetarian dish. Add fruit, coffee, and something simple for anyone who cannot eat the casserole. This is not the brunch reception; if you are hosting a brunch for guests, that is a different meal and a different plan.

Top picks

  • Best for most at-home getting-ready mornings: one or two pans of vegetarian strata, fruit, coffee, and water, ready before hair and makeup start in earnest.
  • Best if some people want meat: the same strata in a second labeled pan, with cooked sausage or bacon folded in, not mixed into the only dish.
  • Best if the house cannot handle a bake that morning: a drop-off breakfast, good bread and fruit, or a nearby pickup, rather than a casserole competing with a steamer and a photographer.

Who you're feeding, and what this meal is not

The morning of a backyard wedding is already a small crowd: people getting dressed, parents, maybe a florist, a photographer, a friend who came early to move chairs. They need breakfast that can wait on a sideboard, not a menu that turns the kitchen into a short-order diner. Count the actual heads who will be in the house, add a little, and stop. You are not feeding the reception at nine in the morning.

If your reception is itself a brunch, do not cook that meal in the same window as hair and photos. There is already a guide to a brunch wedding reception, including a different make-ahead strata meant for guest service. This post is for the quieter morning beforehand, and the recipe below is a vegetarian-first spinach and roasted-pepper bake, scaled for a house full of helpers, not for a hundred guests at eleven. If your “morning” list really is fifty or a hundred — a large getting-ready house, plus family staying over — the same recipe still holds, in more pans, with oven space as the limiting factor.

Put the food where it will not appear in every photograph you did not want it in, and where a person in a white garment will not have to lean over a saucy pan. A dining table, a kitchen island, or a sideboard down the hall is enough. Paper plates are allowed. Coffee belongs on the same table, with water and a non-dairy option if you know you need one. A full coffee bar can wait until later.

Start breakfast before the first hair appointment, or accept that some people will eat a banana in a doorway. The food should be ready without you. That is the whole reason to assemble it the night before.

A make-ahead strata that can wait

A strata is bread soaked in a savory egg custard, with cheese and a filling, assembled in a pan and baked until it puffs and sets. It is a good wedding-morning dish because the work is mostly the day before: roast the peppers, wilt the spinach, cube the bread, mix the custard, and let the pan sit in the refrigerator overnight. In the morning you bake. You do not stand at a stove cracking eggs to order.

This version leads with vegetables. Roasted red peppers, spinach, and a little onion go into a sourdough or country-bread base with a sharp cheese, mustard, and a pinch of nutmeg in the custard. It should taste like a breakfast you would make on a slow Sunday, not like a generic egg bake. If you want meat, cook sausage or bacon separately, drain it well, and fold it into its own pan of the same custard and bread, clearly labeled. Do not plant meat down one side of a single pan and call the other side vegetarian.

Use sheet pans to roast the peppers and to hold bread cubes if you want them a little dry, and bake the strata itself in 9-by-13-inch dishes or in hotel pans if you are cooking for a large list. An instant-read thermometer is the simplest way to know the center is done. Public food-safety guidance for egg dishes is to cook them to 160°F in the center. If you bake ahead and reheat, public guidance is to reheat to 165°F. If you need to hold the pans while people dribble through the kitchen, keep them hot — at or above 140°F — in a low oven or in a chafing dish that actually has fuel in it. Do not leave a deep egg casserole on a warm buffet for a long stretch and hope for the best.

For fifty brunch-sized servings you will need several pans and a serious oven plan; for a typical getting-ready morning you may only need two pans and can treat the “for 50” column as a large family weekend, or cut it down. The recipe is written to the same 50 and 100 scale as the other crowd dishes on this site, so you can cook for a full house if that is honestly the list. A kitchen towel over a pan that is resting, and a skillet only for cooking the onions and any meat, will keep the project from taking every burner at once. A slow cooker is useful for oatmeal or for holding cooked sausage, not for baking the strata.

Spinach and roasted-pepper breakfast strata

Use a sturdy bread that can take a soak: sourdough, a country loaf, or a similar loaf that is a little dry. Fresh, very soft sandwich bread will collapse. Cube it the night before, or earlier, and leave it out for an hour if it is very moist. Roast the peppers until they blister, peel them if the skins are tough, and tear them into strips. Wilt the spinach in a wide pan, or use frozen spinach that you have thawed and squeezed very dry — wet spinach will make a watery center.

The custard is eggs, milk, a spoonful of Dijon, salt, pepper, and a little nutmeg. Pour it over the bread, vegetables, and most of the cheese, press the bread down so it soaks, and let the pan rest in the refrigerator overnight, covered. In the morning, take the pans out while the oven heats to 350°F, scatter the remaining cheese, and bake until the center is set and registers 160°F, and the top is browned. If the top darkens too fast, tent it loosely with foil. Rest the pan ten minutes before you cut it, or it will slump onto the plate.

For a meat pan, brown the sausage or bake the bacon until crisp, drain it, and layer it with the bread and vegetables in its own dish, using the same custard. Label the pans before they go on the table — “spinach and pepper,” “spinach, pepper, and sausage” — not “the other one.” Guests with an egg or dairy allergy will need something else: fruit, a labeled bread, or a dish you have actually planned, not an apology.

Spinach and Roasted-Pepper Breakfast Strata for a Wedding Morning

A vegetarian overnight strata of sourdough, spinach, roasted peppers, and cheese, baked until the center reaches 160°F. The smaller yield is five pans, about fifty servings; double the pans for a hundred. Bake any meat version in its own labeled dish.

Five 9-by-13-inch pans, about 50 servings (double for 100) · Prep 45 minutes · Bake 55 to 75 minutes · Total about 2 hours of work, plus overnight soaking

  1. The day before, roast the peppers until blistered, peel if needed, and tear into strips. Cook the onions in a little oil until soft. Wilt the spinach, or squeeze thawed frozen spinach very dry. Cube the bread.
  2. Butter or oil the pans. Divide the bread, peppers, spinach, onions, and most of the cheese among the pans. If you are making meat pans, add the cooked, drained meat only to those dishes and keep them separate.
  3. Whisk the eggs, milk, mustard, salt, pepper, and nutmeg. Pour the custard evenly over the pans, pressing the bread down so it soaks. Cover and refrigerate overnight.
  4. In the morning, take the pans out while the oven heats to 350°F. Scatter the remaining cheese on top. Bake, uncovered, until the center is set and registers 160°F, usually 55 to 75 minutes depending on the pan and how cold it started. Tent with foil if the top browns too quickly.
  5. Rest 10 minutes, then cut into squares. If you need to hold the pans, keep them at or above 140°F in a low oven or a heated chafer. If you baked ahead and are reheating, public guidance is to reheat to 165°F. Label every pan before it reaches the table.

This is a getting-ready breakfast, or a large-house morning meal, not a substitute for a reception brunch unless you have the oven space and the staff to treat it like one. Cool leftovers in shallow containers and refrigerate. Do not leave an egg casserole sitting out through a long photo session. People who do not eat eggs or dairy need another option you have actually set out.

The rest of the table, and a kitchen that still has to work

Fruit, yogurt, a loaf of bread, and butter will do more for picky or rushed eaters than a second hot dish. Put out water glasses, not only coffee. If you have a getting-ready plan, decide which room is for food and which is for clothes, and keep the two from colliding. A steamer and a strata should not share a tiny galley at the same minute.

Name a person who is not in the wedding clothes to put the pans in the oven, check the thermometer, and cut the first squares. Write the oven time down. A casserole that needed seventy minutes will not be ready because someone hoped it would be. If oven space is tight, bake two pans, hold them correctly, and rotate the rest — or cook for the actual smaller list.

Soak the pans and leave the rest for after the ceremony if you have to. Grease on a formal garment is a worse problem than a pan left to soak.

A short wedding-morning breakfast checklist

Two days out, shop, roast the peppers if you want to be ahead, and confirm how many people will actually be in the house. The night before, assemble the pans, make coffee plan, and clear a table. In the morning, bake early, check 160°F in the center, label the pans, and keep them hot if service will drag.

Stop cooking before you need to get dressed. Leftovers can be packed for later, or sent with family, once they have been handled safely. The goal is a calm plate in a busy house, not a second event. A vegetarian strata that was assembled the night before, a labeled meat pan if you want one, fruit, and coffee will do that job. A skillet of scrambled eggs assigned to a bridesmaid will not. Choose the version of the morning that lets the people in the wedding be in the wedding, and let breakfast be ready without them.

If you later feed guests at a brunch reception, keep the two menus and timelines distinct. What this breakfast should not be is an accidental dress rehearsal that leaves a casserole in the oven when you need to go outside.

Write the bake time on a note on the oven door, and then walk away. If you cannot walk away, simplify the menu until you can.
