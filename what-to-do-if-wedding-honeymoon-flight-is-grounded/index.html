What to Do If Your Destination Wedding or Honeymoon Flight Is Grounded

The nationwide ground stop on Friday, November 13, 2026, has been lifted, but canceled and delayed flights will take time to untangle. If you are supposed to be at a destination wedding or on a honeymoon, you do not need a new plan for every rumor. You need a clear next step, and a calm one.

The short version. Open the airline app and your email before you get in a customer-service line. If your flight is canceled, the U.S. Department of Transportation says you are entitled to a refund if you choose not to travel, even on a nonrefundable ticket — and airlines often offer a rebooking instead. Pick one: a new itinerary that still gets you there, or the money back. Check travel insurance against the policy you actually bought, not a general promise. Then tell the venue, the officiant, and the people waiting for you what you are doing, in one message.

What happened on November 13, 2026

On Friday afternoon, the Federal Aviation Administration issued a nationwide ground stop at U.S. airports after communication issues at multiple Air Route Traffic Control Centers, which the agency described as a temporary telecommunications outage. The FAA's statement said technicians were investigating. The Department of Homeland Security said there were no signs the outage was a cyberattack, according to Reuters.

The ground stop lasted more than four hours and was lifted shortly after 8 p.m. Eastern, The Washington Post reported. It was the longest nationwide shutdown since the September 11, 2001, attacks, and only the second nationwide ground stop since then; the other was in January 2023 and lasted about 90 minutes, Reuters reported.

According to FlightRadar24, cited by Reuters, more than 4,700 flights were canceled and 10,000 delayed on Friday, and another 700 had already been canceled for Saturday. Aviation analysts told Reuters it could take days for airlines to restore their schedules. That is the part that matters for a wedding or a honeymoon: the stop itself is over, and the backlog is not.

If you are in the airport, or still at home

Start with the airline's app, text messages, and email. A cancellation or a new itinerary often lands there before it lands at the ticket counter, and the line at the counter will be long this weekend. Screenshot every notice. Write down the original flight number, the booking code, and the time the message arrived. You will want that paper trail if you ask for a refund, file an insurance claim, or call a venue.

If the airline has already rebooked you, read the new times all the way through: departure, connections, and arrival at the city where you are actually getting married, or actually starting the honeymoon. A seat on a plane is only useful if you still arrive in time to do the thing you flew for. If the new itinerary is worse than not going, you do not have to accept it.

Charge the phone you are using to do all of this. A high-capacity power bank in the carry-on is unglamorous and, on a day like this, more useful than almost anything else in the bag. If you are not yet at the airport, stay home until you have a confirmed seat or a confirmed decision to refund. Sitting on the terminal floor for four hours does not make a canceled flight take off.

Rebooking, or a refund

This is the decision most couples need, and it is clearer than it feels in a noisy terminal. Under U.S. Department of Transportation refund rules, if an airline cancels your flight, you are entitled to a refund of the airfare, including taxes and ancillary fees, if you choose not to travel and you do not accept a travel credit, voucher, or other compensation. That is true even if the ticket was sold as nonrefundable, and it does not depend on why the flight was canceled.

The same refund right applies if the airline significantly delays or changes the flight and you choose not to take the new plan. DOT's definition, also in 14 CFR 260.2, includes a departure or arrival that moves by three hours or more on a domestic itinerary, or six hours or more on an international one; a change of origin or destination airport; more connections than you originally booked; an involuntary downgrade; and certain changes that affect accessibility for a passenger with a disability.

Airlines are not required by DOT to rebook you, but they often will. When they offer a new flight, they must tell you that you can have a refund instead. If you want to travel, accept the rebooking before any deadline they set — they are allowed to put a clock on the offer. If you want the money back, decline the new flight and decline the voucher. If you simply take the rebooked flight, you are not entitled to a refund under the DOT rule. Keep the passport and the confirmations in a passport holder in your personal item, not in a bag you might have to check at the last minute.

If you bought the ticket directly from the airline and you do not accept the alternative, the refund should be automatic, to the original form of payment: within seven business days for a credit card, and within 20 calendar days for cash, check, or many other forms of payment. If you booked through a third party, contact that agency as well as the airline, and use the DOT page as your reference. If you have trouble getting a refund you believe you are owed, you can file a complaint with the Department of Transportation.

One thing the federal refund rule does not do: it does not, by itself, require the airline to pay for a hotel, meals, or a rental car while you wait. Ask what the airline is offering. Treat overnight help as something they may provide, not something to count on in the same way as the refund.

Travel insurance, read against the policy you have

Do not assume a policy covers this, and do not assume it doesn't. Open the documents you were sent when you bought the plan and look for trip delay, trip interruption, and trip cancellation. An air-traffic-control outage or an airline cancellation is often a reason a delay benefit can help with meals or a last-minute room, and it may be a reason you can interrupt or cancel a trip you can no longer take. The wording varies, and so do waiting periods and caps.

Call the insurer with the airline cancellation or delay notice in front of you. Ask what they need in writing, and whether a refunded airline ticket changes the claim. If you already asked the airline for a refund, tell the insurer that too. A policy is a contract, not a vibe, and a five-minute call now is worth more than a hopeful reread on Monday.

If this was the wedding trip, or the honeymoon

A destination wedding and a honeymoon look the same at the gate, and they are not the same problem. If you are the couple and the ceremony is in the next day or two, the first call after the airline is the officiant, the venue, and whoever is holding the day-of timeline. Tell them the new arrival time, or that you cannot make it, before you spend an hour comparing routing apps. A short delay can mean a later ceremony with the people who are already in town. A missed weekend may mean moving vows, or signing papers locally and gathering later. Our guides to a backyard elopement and a post-elopement reception are useful when the big original plan no longer fits the week you are in.

If guests are the ones in the air, or supposed to be, that is a different job, and we wrote it out separately: how to help wedding guests when flights are grounded. You cannot rebook 80 tickets. You can tell people, clearly, what is happening, and how they can still be part of the day.

If this was the honeymoon, be honest about whether a two-day-late arrival is still the trip you paid for. A rebooking that lands you at the hotel for half the nights can be worth taking. A rebooking that burns the whole week may be a refund, a later trip, and a quieter few days at home. There is no prize for forcing a celebration through a connection you will miss.

A carry-on that can finish the trip

If you accept a new flight, assume checked bags will be slower than you are, and that a missed connection can separate you from a dress or a suit. Put the ceremony clothes — or at least one complete outfit you could get married in — in a weekender or carry-on you do not gate-check. Rings, documents, medicines, and a charger go in the same bag. Put a luggage tag on every bag you check, and an AirTag in anything you cannot replace this weekend.

If driving has become the better option, that is a real plan, not a failure. Measure the drive against the ceremony time with a margin, not against a hopeful map. Sleep is part of arriving. What to skip

Skip buying a second full-fare ticket on another airline before you have asked the original airline to rebook you, unless you are sure you can afford to risk two tickets. Skip accepting a voucher if what you want is a refund; you can say no. Skip announcing a cancellation of the wedding to every guest before you have spoken to the venue. Skip arguing with a gate agent about whose fault a telecommunications outage was. And skip refreshing social media for official guidance. The FAA, the airline, and the DOT refunds page are the sources that matter.

A short checklist for a grounded wedding or honeymoon flight

Confirm the actual status in the airline app and your email, and save the notices. Decide, in writing, whether you are taking a rebooking or asking for a refund. Check the DOT definitions if the flight was delayed or changed rather than canceled. Call the travel insurer with the airline notice in hand. Tell the venue, the officiant, and your families what you are doing. If you still travel, keep the irreplaceable things in the bag on your shoulder. Then take the next confirmed step, not the noisiest one.

Questions couples actually ask

Do I get a refund if my wedding or honeymoon flight was canceled in the ground stop?

If the airline canceled the flight and you choose not to travel or accept a voucher, credit, or other compensation, yes — the U.S. Department of Transportation says you are entitled to a refund of the airfare, including taxes and fees, even on a nonrefundable ticket. The reason for the cancellation does not change that. See the DOT refunds page.

What if my flight was not canceled, just delayed for hours?

A significant delay or change can also trigger the same refund right if you choose not to take the new itinerary. For domestic trips, a departure or arrival that moves by three hours or more is one of the thresholds; for international trips it is six hours. A different airport, extra connections, or an involuntary downgrade can also qualify. The details are on the DOT page and in 14 CFR 260.2.

Will the airline pay for a hotel if I am stranded overnight?

Do not count on it as a federal right the way a ticket refund is. Ask the airline what they are offering for a room, meals, or a rebooking on another carrier. Some will help; some will not. Your travel insurance, if you have a trip-delay benefit, is often the more reliable place to look, and only if the policy you bought actually says so.

Should we postpone the wedding today?

Not automatically. If you and enough of the key people can still arrive, a later ceremony, a smaller local guest list, or a livestream for the rest may be enough. If you cannot arrive, talk to the venue and the officiant before you send a blanket postponement. A clear delay is kinder than a silence, and kinder than a final cancellation you have to walk back in the morning.
