Thermacell and Mosquito Dunks: A Backyard Wedding Bug Plan That Is More Than Candles

Citronella candles will not carry a backyard wedding. A real bug plan starts a week out, with standing water and a granular treatment, and it continues on the day with a tested perimeter repeller and a labeled wipe station people can choose. Never spray the guests.

The short version. A week before the wedding, walk the yard, empty saucers and gutters, and put mosquito dunks in any water that has to stay. The day of, set a few Thermacell Patio Shields around the ceremony and cocktail area after you have tested one in that yard, at dusk. Offer repellent wipes at a labeled station. Cover the food. Candles can be pretty. They are not the plan. For the longer yard essay, see our guide to bugs at an outdoor wedding; this post is the kit.

Start with the water, a week out

Mosquitoes do not appear at cocktail hour from nowhere. They come from standing water that has been sitting in the yard, the gutters, a birdbath, a clogged drain, a plant saucer, a forgotten bucket, a wheelbarrow, or the toys in a neighbor's yard you cannot do much about. The most effective work you will do is ordinary, and it happens days before anyone arrives in a dress.

Walk the property with a hose and a trash bag. Empty what you can. For water that has to stay — a rain barrel, a pond, a birdbath you will refill — use mosquito dunks, which treat the water with a bacteria (Bti) that targets mosquito larvae and is the standard advice from extension offices and from couples who have already had this fight. Mosquito bits are the granular version for smaller, shallower spots and plant trays. Follow the label. This is a yard treatment, not a decoration, and it needs a little time to work.

Mow a few days ahead, not the morning of, so the clippings can dry and the yard is not freshly stirred up as guests arrive. If there is a swampy corner or a ditch you do not control, be honest about it when you place the ceremony and the cocktail hour. You can move a chair. You cannot move a wetland.

A tested repeller, not a ring of candles you promise will work

Citronella has a reputation it has not earned at the scale of a wedding. A few candles on tables can make a porch feel finished and may help a little in still air, right next to the flame. They will not clear a lawn. Forum threads about outdoor wedding bugs come back to this with some irritation: the candles were pretty, and the mosquitoes were unimpressed.

A Thermacell Patio Shield is the device people mention when they have actually found something that helped in a real yard. It makes a small zone of repellent in the air, it is relatively quiet, and it does not smell like a candle shop. It is not magic, and it is not a force field around a 200-foot lawn. You need enough units for the areas where people will stand and sit — the ceremony rows, the cocktail pocket, the bar — and you need to turn them on early enough that the zone has formed before guests arrive.

Test one in that yard, at dusk, a week or two ahead, on a still evening. If it does nothing in your particular microclimate, you want to know now, while you can add more units, move the ceremony, or lean harder on the wipe station. Buy a refill kit so you are not out of mats at 7 p.m. Place the units at the edge of the gathering, not in the middle of the aisle, and not where a child can pick one up. Keep citronella torches if you like the look, and be honest with yourself about what they are: atmosphere, and maybe a little help along a path, not the plan.

A wipe station people can choose, and food that stays covered

Some guests will want a repellent, and some will not, including anyone who is pregnant, sensitive to scents, or holding a baby. Do not walk around spraying people. Do not put a hose-end sprayer on a timer and call it hospitality. Set a small table or a labeled basket of picaridin wipes at the entrance to the yard or just beside the welcome drinks, with a sign that says they are there if you want them. Picaridin is the active ingredient many people prefer for a wedding because it is effective and usually less heavily scented than some DEET sprays, but the brand matters less than the fact that it is a wipe, and optional.

Cover the food. A set of mesh food covers on a buffet is unglamorous and essential. Flies and wasps care about the cake and the fruit more than they care about your floral color. Keep trash closed, and move empty plates. A fire pit later in the evening can help a little with bugs at the edge of the party; it can also draw people into the smoke. If you have one, see our fire-pit cocktail hour notes and do not rely on it as the mosquito plan.

Placement, dusk, and what you cannot control

Dusk is the worst hour, and it is also when a lot of ceremonies and cocktail hours look their best. If you know the yard is buggy at sunset, consider a slightly earlier ceremony, or move cocktail hour to a porch, a tent with sides rolled down, or the side of the house with a breeze. Fans help more than people think, not as a repellent but as moving air mosquitoes are less fond of crossing. A couple of existing patio fans, aimed across a seating area, are worth plugging in.

Tell the caterer and the bartender about the wipe station and the covers, so they are not fighting wasps with a towel. Tell the photographer too; they will be standing still in the grass longer than almost anyone, and they will be glad you were honest.

You cannot control the next-door ditch or a wet summer. You can put the cake inside until it is time to cut it, you can give people a choice about wipes, and you can avoid promising a bug-free evening in the invitation. A light, truthful line — "we'll be outdoors at dusk; we will have wraps and a bug station" — is plenty.

If someone at the wedding has a known serious reaction to stings, this is a conversation with them ahead of time, not a surprise on the lawn. Know where the nearest quiet indoor room is, and who has any emergency medication they already carry. A basket of wipes is not that plan.

What to skip

Skip a ring of citronella candles as the entire strategy. Skip spraying guests, spraying the cake table, or setting off a backyard fogger an hour before people arrive unless you know exactly what you are doing and you have somewhere else for people to be while it settles. Skip unboxing a repeller as the processional starts. Skip leaving standing water "because it looks pretty." And skip pretending a buggy yard will be fine because the invitations already went out. The week-out walk is still available to you.

A short backyard-bug-kit checklist

A week out, empty the standing water and treat what you cannot empty. Test a zone repeller in that yard at dusk, and buy refills. On the day, start the units early, cover the food, and set out a labeled wipe station people can walk past or use. Keep candles for the look if you want them. Then let the evening be a comfortable one, not a story about the mosquitoes.
