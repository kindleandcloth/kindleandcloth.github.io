The bathroom basket at a wedding is only useful if it holds what people actually reach for. Hydrocolloid blister pads, a stain pen, a tiny sewing kit, tampons, and deodorant will do more than a forty-item Pinterest list. Keep it small, keep bug spray out of the stall, and set one basket in each restroom.

The short version. Build a short basket: blister pads, a Tide To Go, a mini sewing kit, tampons and pads, deodorant, a lint roller, safety pins, and bobby pins. Put it in a low woven basket with a small sign, in every restroom guests will use, including the portable ones. Leave the bug spray, the perfume, and the hairspray outside the stall, or skip them. Check the basket at cocktail hour and after dinner, and do not let it become a science project of opened products.

What people actually take from the basket

Ask around in wedding planning threads about bathroom baskets and you get a very short list, repeated with feeling. Blister pads. Tampons. A stain pen. Safety pins or a needle and thread. Deodorant. Occasionally a lint roller, a bobby pin, or a hair elastic. Almost nobody writes in to say they were saved by a travel-size perfume, a full dental kit, or a bottle of mouthwash they did not want to use in a portable restroom.

That is good news. It means you can build a basket in fifteen minutes, for little money, and it will be more useful than a styled tray of forty products. The point is not to impress anyone with how complete your hospitality is. The point is that a guest with a blister, a popped seam, or an unexpected period can fix it and come back to the party.

Here's the honest part. You will not know everything someone might need, and you should not try. A small first-aid add-on is fine. A pharmacy on a folding table is not. If you are also putting together a pouch that travels with the wedding party, see our wedding day emergency kit — that is a different, shorter list, and it should not live only in the bathroom.

The short list, and why each thing is on it

Blister pads. Start with hydrocolloid blister pads, not a box of plastic bandages. Guests in new shoes, on grass, gravel, or a dance floor, get hot spots by cocktail hour. A pad that stays on and reduces friction is the one they will be grateful for. Put the box in the basket unopened, with a pair of small scissors if the pads need cutting, or buy a size that does not.

A stain pen and a sewing kit. Red wine, a dropped appetizer, a loose button, a strap that will not stay up: this is the everyday emergency list. A Tide To Go and a SINGER mini sewing kit cover most of it. Add a box of assorted safety pins even if the kit already has a few; people can find a pin when they cannot thread a needle in a dim restroom. A pack of unscented makeup wipes helps with mascara and with the same stains on skin.

Period products, deodorant, and hair. A variety pack of tampons and pads is not optional, and it belongs in every restroom, not only the one you think of as the women's room. A travel-size deodorant, unscented if you can find it, and a pack of bobby pins will be used. A lint roller is the last of the "almost always useful" items, especially at a wedding with dark suits and a lot of hugging.

How to set it up so it feels like a wedding bathroom

A small woven basket is enough of a container. Line it with a cloth napkin if you want it to look finished, and add a small card that says "please help yourself." You do not need a calligraphy sign, and you do not need to put every item in a labeled jar. People in a hurry will dump the jar.

Set one basket in each restroom guests will use. At a house, that may be two bathrooms; put a basket in both, even if one feels like "the family one." At a backyard with rented restrooms, the basket is part of what makes a portable stall feel like a wedding and not a job site. Our guide to backyard wedding restrooms covers placement, lighting, and the unglamorous restocking of paper and soap. The comfort basket sits on top of that, not instead of it.

Keep aerosols and strong scents out of a small stall. Hairspray, perfume, and bug spray linger, and they are rough on anyone who is pregnant, scent-sensitive, or already warm. If you want bug wipes, set them on a table outside, labeled, which is the same advice we give in the bugs guide. Sunscreen belongs there too, not next to the hand towels.

Restocking, portable restrooms, and a few kind extras

Check the baskets at the end of cocktail hour and once after dinner. Blister pads and period products are the first to go. Assign this to the same person who is checking paper and soap, or to a friend who already has a reason to walk the yard. Put backup supplies in a closed bin in the house or a nearby shed, not in a bag on the bathroom floor.

In a rented restroom, use a basket that will not tip on a narrow shelf, and do not leave anything that will melt or spill in a hot stall. A stain pen and pads are fine. A chocolate, a candle, and a glass bottle of lotion are not. If the stall is tight, hang a small bag on a hook instead of using a basket, and keep the list even shorter.

If children are at the wedding, a small thing of plain unscented wipes and a couple of bandages with a tame pattern will be used, and they can live in the same basket. Keep any medications, including pain relievers and allergy tablets, with a person, not in an unsupervised bathroom. You cannot know another guest's medical situation from a basket, and you should not try to.

The getting-ready room can have its own, slightly different, tray: fashion tape, a steamer, the same stain pen. That is covered in getting ready at home. Do not assume the bathroom basket will be visited by the wedding party in time to fix a strap. They need the pouch in the room with them.

A note on wording, privacy, and not making it weird

The sign should be small and matter-of-fact. "A few things you might need — please help yourself" is enough. You do not need to list every item on the card, and you do not need a joke. Bathroom humor on a framed sign is a choice, and most guests, in the moment they need a tampon or a stain pen, would rather not read one.

Place the basket where it can be used without an audience. On a sink, on a small shelf, or on a stool beside the mirror is better than in the path of the door. In a portable restroom, a hook or a high corner shelf beats the floor. The goal is that someone can take a pad or a blister plaster without carrying it across a room of people.

If you are worried about waste, open nothing in advance except the lint roller. People will take a sealed pack of pads over a basket of loose ones, and you will throw away less. Put a small trash can with a lid in every restroom if there is not already one, and check it when you restock. A useful basket in a room with no trash can is only half done.

What to skip

Skip the forty-item list. Skip perfume, open hairspray, and bug spray in the stall. Skip anything you would be sad to see used, dumped, or taken home in a purse. Skip medications in an unsupervised basket. Skip a basket only in "the nice bathroom" when guests will also use a portable stall across the lawn. And skip checking it for the first time at midnight, when the blister pads are long gone and someone has already danced in pain for an hour.

A short bathroom-basket checklist

Buy blister pads, a stain pen, a mini sewing kit, safety pins, a period-product variety pack, deodorant, bobby pins, a lint roller, and unscented wipes. Put them in a small woven basket with a plain "help yourself" card, in every restroom. Leave scented sprays and bug products outside. Restock at cocktail hour and after dinner from a closed backup bin. That is the whole project, and it is one of the kindest ones you will do.
