Decor & DIY

Outdoor lighting, flowers, centerpieces, arches, signs, seating, photo booths, invitations, and other backyard wedding decor you can actually finish.

Decor for a backyard wedding should survive the weather, sit in a place people can walk, and still leave room for a plate. The guides here are for lighting a yard after dusk, setting a table, building or borrowing an arch, making a sign people can follow, and doing a few projects without turning the week before into a second job.

Paper belongs here too: invitations, save-the-dates, and a guestbook that is not a pile of loose cards in the wind. If you are choosing chairs, a dance floor, or heaters, those decisions are as much planning as they are looks — you will find them here and on the planning hub, where the logistics are spelled out.

    An outdoor wedding lighting plan that still works after dark: three layers (overhead, table, and paths), how to hang and power them, and a simple night-test timeline.

  • Outdoor Wedding Decor Ideas

    Outdoor wedding decor ideas that survive wind, heat, and sunset: long-table centerpieces, hardy florals, three layers of lighting, signs, and comfort stations.

  • Eco-Friendly Wedding Decor

    Eco-friendly wedding decor is less about new green products and more about using fewer things harder: rent, borrow, buy only what you'll keep, and know where it all goes on Sunday.

  • A Sunset Backyard Wedding

    Look up sunset for your town and date, start the ceremony early enough for vows and photographs, and plan lights, a layer, and a meal that does not depend on the last glow.

  • A Zero-Waste Backyard Wedding

    How to plan a zero-waste (or honestly low-waste) backyard wedding: rent real dishes, use cloth, check what your town composts, label the bins, and pack leftovers before the last toast.
