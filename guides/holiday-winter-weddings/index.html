Guides for

Holiday & Winter Weddings

Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year's, January, and Valentine's backyard weddings, plus how to keep people warm, dressed, and fed in the cold months.

A holiday or winter wedding can use the travel people are already doing, the house you already have, and a calendar that is not June. It can also collide with someone else's feast, a closed courthouse, or a yard that is dark at four. These guides are written for the month you are actually in — Thanksgiving week, a Christmas house, New Year's Eve or Day, a quiet January, a Valentine's date in February — without turning the day into a theme.

Warmth, dressing, and winter food have their own posts, because a short outdoor vow is only a good idea if there is a real room, a coat, and a meal waiting afterward. Gift guides that belong to this season live here too, and in full on the gifts hub.

Thanksgiving week

A wedding, a rehearsal dinner, or an announcement, without a second Thursday feast.

  • A Thanksgiving Weekend Wedding

    A Thanksgiving weekend wedding at home can use the travel people are already doing. Keep the ceremony short, serve a meal that is not a second Thursday dinner, and end in time for Sunday flights.

  • A Thanksgiving Rehearsal Dinner

    Use a holiday gathering as a rehearsal dinner without turning the night into Thanksgiving twice: a smaller list, a make-ahead main, and a clear end.

  • A Thanksgiving Engagement Announcement

    Tell the closest people first, keep any table announcement short, and let a card or a message follow later. A holiday meal should still be allowed to be a meal.

Christmas and December

A wedding at home, a first Christmas, and the small gifts of the season.

  • A Christmas Wedding at Home

    A Christmas wedding at home that uses the tree, the lights, and the table you already have — plus a clear plan for dates, travel, a warm room, and a meal that still feels like a wedding.

  • A First Christmas as Newlyweds

    How to spend a first Christmas as a married couple: family logistics first, one or two small traditions, and a day that does not have to prove anything.

  • Stocking Stuffers for the Bride and Groom

    Stocking stuffers for a bride, a groom, or a newly engaged couple: small, useful things — socks, a travel case, a tumbler — and what to leave out of the cuff.

  • Holiday Gifts for an Engaged Couple

    Holiday gifts for a newly engaged couple that they will actually use: a ring dish, a planning notebook, a skillet, or an experience — and why to skip another pair of flutes.

  • Winter Wedding Favors

    Winter wedding favors that do a job in the cold: hand warmers, cocoa kits, and sturdy treats, plus when to skip a take-home and put the money into comfort instead.

  • Black Friday Wedding Planning Deals

    How to use Black Friday and Cyber Monday for a backyard wedding: a list first, measurements, categories that usually go on sale, and a way to compare without prices that go stale.

New Year's and January

A midnight, a quieter morning after, or a month that is still winter.

  • A New Year's Eve Wedding at Home

    A small New Year's Eve wedding at home: decide what midnight is for, plan around early dusk and a way home, and run the toast as a real plan, not a scramble.

  • A New Year's Day Wedding

    A New Year's Day wedding can be a late-morning ceremony and a lunch. Do the license before December 31, keep the day short, and skip the countdown.

  • A January Backyard Wedding

    A January backyard wedding can use winter light and a house that is already closed in. Keep outdoor vows short, do the legal work for the year you are in, and serve a meal indoors at a civil hour.

Valentine's Day and a winter elopement

A date in February, or a short outdoor vow with a warm after.

  • A Valentine's Day Backyard Wedding

    How to have a Valentine's Day backyard wedding: a short outdoor vow, a warm house, a list the room can hold, and a little seasonal color without a costume.

  • A Winter Elopement

    How to elope in winter: a short outdoor vow, a warm indoor backup, legal timing, and a meal after — distinct from a general backyard elopement.
