Wedding Food & Recipes

Practical wedding food ideas for a backyard or a house: bars, tray dinners, brunch, dessert, drinks, and crowd-scaled recipes you can actually hold.

Food is where a backyard wedding either feels generous or feels like a scramble. The guides here are written for a real list, a real kitchen, and a way to keep the food hot — or briefly held — without a banquet staff. If you want a station, start with a bar or a buffet plan. If you want a sit-down meal, start with a tray dinner or a family-style table. If you want a recipe, look for the printable card in the post.

None of these is a tasting menu of twelve ideas. Each post picks one way to feed people and stays with it: how much to make, how to label it, and what to skip. Drinks and dessert have their own guides, so the dinner does not have to do every job.

Bowl dinners and food bars

A line, a ladle or a pan, and a topping list short enough to finish.

  • A Wedding Soup Bar

    How to run a wedding soup bar: one or two pots held hot, enough bread, and a roasted tomato soup from canned tomatoes that can be made ahead.

  • A Wedding Chili Bar

    Treat chili as the main, not a topping: a white chicken chili you can make ahead, a labeled bean pot if you need one, and cornbread on the side.

  • A Baked Potato Bar

    How to run a wedding potato bar: oven-baked potatoes held hot, a short topping line, and a slow-cooker bean chili that makes the plate a meal.

  • A Wedding Taco Bar

    How to set up a wedding taco bar: how much food to make, which fillings and toppings, how to keep it hot, and how to run a line that doesn't stall the evening.

  • A Wedding Sandwich Bar

    Focaccia or ciabatta slabs, filled and cut into squares: one chicken or Italian slab and a labeled roasted-vegetable slab. Two slabs feed about 24; four, about 48.

  • Wedding Pasta Bar

    How to run a wedding pasta bar that works: two sauces, pasta cooked in batches, a helper at the chafer, and a make-ahead Sunday gravy for a crowd.

  • A Wedding Mac and Cheese Bar

    How to run a wedding mac and cheese bar: a holdable cheddar-and-gruyère baked mac, a short topping line, and pans that stay hot.

  • A Pizza Wedding Reception

    A pizza wedding reception that works: how many pies to order, how to keep them hot, a real salad, and how to talk to a shop or a wood-fired cook.

Tray dinners and sit-down mains

Pans you can make ahead, and a table that can actually hold a meal.

  • Baked Ziti for a Wedding Crowd

    Make-ahead baked ziti in 9x13 pans, with a labeled vegetarian tray if you need one, and sausage kept in its own dishes.

  • Wedding Lasagna

    Make-ahead lasagna in 9x13 pans that you can bake, rest, and slice. Two pans feed about 24 as a main; four pans, about 48. Keep a vegetarian pan labeled and separate.

  • Wedding Pot Roast

    Chuck browned and braised until fork-tender, with gravy from the juices. Two 4-pound roasts feed about 24; four feed about 48. Make ahead and reheat to 165°F.

  • Wedding Fried Chicken

    Buttermilk oven-fried chicken on a rack over sheet pans, cooked to 165°F and held briefly uncovered. Two sheets hold about 24 pieces; 48 takes a second wave.

  • A Vegetarian Wedding Dinner

    A vegetarian wedding main that is actually dinner: a mushroom-and-lentil cottage pie you can assemble ahead and bake in 9x13 pans.

  • Family-Style Wedding Dinner

    How to serve a family-style wedding dinner: tables that fit the platters, a menu that can wait, helpers in a wave, and chicken cacciatore for a crowd.

  • A Small Wedding Dinner at Home

    How to host a small wedding dinner at home: a hard headcount, who cooks, a table that fits the rooms you have, a menu that works in a house kitchen, and a calm end to the night.

  • Winter Wedding Food Ideas

    Winter wedding food that can wait: a short warm menu, holds and timing for an early dark, and a roasted butternut squash soup you can make ahead.

  • A Backyard BBQ Wedding Menu

    A backyard BBQ wedding menu that can wait: what to serve, whether to hire a pit cook or use a drop-off, and how to keep the meat hot, the line moving, and the two of you out of the smoke.

  • Budget Wedding Food Ideas

    Budget wedding food ideas that work in a yard: drop-off catering, food trucks, family-style, stations, brunch, drinks, and a service timeline for helpers.

  • Wedding Macaroni Salad

    Elbow pasta with a mayonnaise dressing loosened with vinegar or pickle brine and mustard, made ahead and kept cold. Two pounds of dry pasta serve about 24 as a side; four pounds, about 48.

  • Wedding Potato Salad

    Boiled potatoes, a mayonnaise dressing with mustard, celery and onion, made ahead and kept cold. About 6 pounds of potatoes serve 24 as a side; about 12 pounds, 48.

  • Wedding Coleslaw

    Shredded cabbage and carrot in a cider-vinegar dressing, made ahead and kept cool. About 4 pounds of cabbage serve 24 as a side; about 8 pounds, 48. It is not another mayonnaise salad.

  • Wedding Baked Beans

    Canned beans baked with onion, molasses, and mustard until the pot is thick. About 4 quarts serve 24 as a side; about 8 quarts, 48. Hold at or above 140°F, and label a meatless pot if you make one.

Brunch and the morning

A civil hour, a batter or a casserole, and coffee that does not depend on the cook.

  • A Brunch Wedding Reception

    A brunch wedding reception at home: a timeline, a menu that isn't only pastry, coffee that doesn't run out, and a short plan for a next-day farewell brunch.

  • Wedding-Morning Breakfast

    Breakfast for the people getting ready: a make-ahead vegetarian spinach and roasted-pepper strata, with an optional meat pan that is labeled as its own dish.

  • A Wedding Waffle Bar

    A waffle bar has to be cooked in batches and held only briefly on a rack. Here is a buttermilk batter, a short topping line, and a plan for two irons.

  • A Wedding Pancake Bar

    A buttermilk pancake batter baked on half-sheet pans and cut into squares, not flipped one by one. Two pans feed about 24 as a brunch main; four pans, about 48.

  • A Wedding Biscuit Bar

    Buttermilk biscuits baked on half-sheets, sausage gravy held hot in a Dutch oven, and a labeled butter-and-jam path. About 24 biscuits on two pans, or 48 on four.

  • A Farewell Brunch After the Wedding

    A farewell brunch is a shorter, smaller morning for people who stayed or traveled, not a second reception and not the wedding-morning breakfast.

  • Wedding Cinnamon Rolls

    A slightly enriched yeast dough baked in 9x13 pans and iced close to service. Two pans make about 24 rolls; four pans, about 48. An overnight cold rise in the pans is optional.

Dessert

A table you can slice, refill, and clear.

  • A Budget Wedding Dessert Table

    A budget wedding dessert table that still feels generous: how much to serve, cake alternatives, shade and bugs, and a display you can actually pull off outdoors.

  • A Wedding Cookie Table

    How to run a wedding cookie table: how many cookies to plan, how to coordinate bakers, and a lemon knot cookie you can bake ahead for a crowd.

  • A Wedding Pie Table

    How to run a wedding pie table: how many pies to plan, bakery versus homemade, and an apple crumb slab pie that slices into squares for a crowd.

  • Wedding Banana Pudding

    A scratch vanilla pudding layered with wafers and bananas close to service, then topped with whipped cream. Two 9x13 pans serve about 24; four pans, about 48.

  • Late-Night Wedding Snacks

    Late-night wedding snacks that work in a yard: what to serve, when to bring it out, how much to make, and how to do it without the two of you ending up back in the kitchen.

  • Wedding Tiramisu

    Espresso-soaked ladyfingers and a mascarpone cream made with whipped cream, not raw yolks. Assemble ahead, keep cold, and dust cocoa close to service. Two 9x13 pans serve about 24; four, about 48.

  • Wedding Peach Cobbler

    Peaches and a biscuit topping, baked in 9x13 pans until the fruit bubbles and the top is browned. Two pans serve about 24; four, about 48. Serve warm.

  • Wedding Tres Leches

    A sponge baked in 9x13 pans, soaked with three milks, topped with whipped cream, and kept cold. Two pans serve about 24; four, about 48.

  • Wedding Lemon Bars

    A shortbread crust and a lemon filling, baked in 9x13 pans, chilled thoroughly, and cut into squares. Two pans serve about 24 generous squares; four, about 48.

  • Wedding Brownies

    A cocoa brownie batter, baked in 9x13 pans until just set, cooled completely, and cut into squares. Two pans serve about 24 generous squares; four, about 48. They are not a cookie table.

  • Wedding Bread Pudding

    Cubed slightly stale bread soaked in a simple custard, baked in 9x13 pans until the center is set, and served warm. Two pans serve about 24; four, about 48. It is not banana pudding, and it is not cobbler.

  • Wedding Cupcakes

    DIY wedding cupcakes: about one to one and a half per guest, baked in batches of 24, frozen ahead, frosted the day before. Includes a vanilla buttermilk cupcake and buttercream recipe.

    A wedding hot cocoa and cider bar that works: how much to make, how to keep it hot, cups and toppings, where to put the station, and who watches the pot.

  • Budget Wedding Bar and Drinks

    A budget wedding bar that holds up: beer, wine, water, and one batched cocktail; how much to buy; ice, shade, glassware, and who pours so the two of you are not stuck behind the table.

  • Non-Alcoholic Wedding Drinks

    How to set a real non-alcoholic drink station at a backyard wedding, with water, a sparkling option, and a make-ahead citrus-cranberry punch finished with ginger ale.

  • Wedding Coffee Bar

    How to set a wedding coffee bar after dinner: hot regular and decaf, an iced option, clear labels, and a make-ahead cold-brew concentrate for a crowd.

  • Wedding Sangria

    Big-batch wedding sangria: red wine, brandy, orange, apple, and cranberry, chilled overnight and topped with sparkling water. About 24 glasses per batch, with an alcohol-free version.

Stations, catering, and how you serve

    A realistic plan for self-catering or semi-catering a backyard wedding: a guest cap, a menu that can wait, drop-off as the middle path, gear, helpers, and a timeline that leaves you at your own party.

  • A Food Truck Wedding

    A food truck wedding, planned like a caterer: access, permits, power, a line that can move, and a quote that includes the hours you actually need.

  • A Wedding Potluck

    How to host a wedding potluck: invitation wording, a sign-up that works, a host main, labels, and a cold chain — plus a baked mac and cheese you can make ahead.

    A backyard wedding buffet that works: table layout, chafing dishes, how long food can sit, how much to serve, and mashed potatoes that hold.

  • A Wedding Grazing Table

    How to build a wedding grazing table that gets eaten, not just photographed: volume, food safety, boards, and when a spread is cocktail hour versus the whole meal.

    A short cocktail-hour appetizer menu for a backyard wedding, with passed or stationed bites, make-ahead timing, and classic stuffed mushrooms for a crowd.

    Plan cheese fondue by the pot: Gruyère and Emmental for tables of eight or ten, a gentle flame, and enough bread. This is not a buffet for a hundred.

    Bake soft pretzels in batches, dip them in a baking-soda bath, and hold them briefly in a low oven. Serve with mustard and one cheese dip. About 24 or 48 pretzels.
